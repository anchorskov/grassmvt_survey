How Surveys Work

This page explains how surveys are created, how people access them, how results are grouped, and why some district-level views may not yet show data.

Intro

Grassroots Movement uses surveys to gather public input by topic, publish grouped results, and connect that input to transparent civic discussion.

How surveys are created

Surveys are created by Jimmy Skovgard, with input and review from volunteers. Questions are reviewed before publication so topics, wording, and revisions can be tracked over time.

How to access surveys

Users can browse and open surveys by topic from the surveys list. Each survey has its own results page, and some surveys also connect to a Town Hall discussion page for public statements and receipts.

Accounts and verified users

Anyone can browse surveys and review public result views. Users who create accounts can save progress, return later, and access account tools. Verified users may also unlock district-based result views tied to the verified address context on the account.

How results are grouped

Results are aggregated by survey name. Depending on available data and verification level, users may review statewide, House district, and Senate district results for the same survey.

Minimum response threshold

Results are shown only when at least 10 responses exist for that survey and filter combination. That threshold applies separately to statewide, House district, and Senate district views.

Why a threshold is used

The threshold helps prevent unstable or misleading small-sample views. Some surveys may have data and some may not. Some House or Senate districts may already have enough responses to show results, while others may still be below the threshold.
